Workplace English story unit covering The Post-Merger Boardroom (executive_lounge).

1. Workplace Listening Script（8 lines）

  1. Mr. Harrington: Thank you all for attending. As the chairperson of this board, I find it imperative that we address the recent acquisition of Sterling Properties immediately.
  2. Victoria: Agreed. The financials are... concerning. We need to know how we plan to absorb the existing debt without downsizing our core operations.
  3. Sophia: Actually, Victoria, this merger is a chance to reposition. We are merging with a prestigious firm that has an influential network in the Asian market.
  4. Mr. Harrington: Repositioning is fine, Sophia, but I need concrete numbers. Alex, as the new executive advisor, walk me through your thinking on the cost synergy.
  5. Victoria: I am listening intently. Please explain how this takeover benefits the bottom line, rather than just expanding our headquarters footprint.
  6. Sophia: Don't be so apprehensive, Victoria. The founder of Sterling was a prominent businessman who built a loyal client base. We just need to oversee the transition carefully.
  7. Mr. Harrington: Let's avoid disagreement on this point. If we cannot merge the IT systems by Q3, we risk a major operational failure. Alex, do you have a timeline?
  8. Victoria: I require a detailed report from the presenter by Friday. Every attendee in this room expects transparency regarding the realtor commissions involved.

EP03 · The Reimbursement Trap

Workplace English story unit covering The Reimbursement Trap (Boardroom / Finance Office).

1. Workplace Listening Script（8 lines）

  1. Victoria: Ms. Okafor, we need to resolve these discrepancies before the close of business.
  2. Ms. Okafor: Could you clarify the outstanding items on this invoice? The receipt does not match the total.
  3. Victoria: The calculation includes a premium for emergency logistics. We had to authorize it immediately.
  4. Ms. Okafor: I understand, but we must audit every transaction thoroughly. Is this economically justifiable?
  5. Mr. Harrington: Well, one might argue that maximizing efficiency sometimes requires a probable increase in cost.
  6. Ms. Okafor: Please subtract the personal expenses from the business overheads in your spreadsheet.
  7. Victoria: I will allocate the remainder of the budget to cover any shortfall. The funds are sufficient.
  8. Ms. Okafor: Ensure the banker receives the corrected withdrawal request by noon tomorrow.

2. Core Episode Reading（article）

Internal Memo: Guidelines for Q3 Expense Reporting and Budget Allocation

All departments must submit their expense reports by Friday. It is crucial to ensure that every invoice and receipt accurately reflects business transactions. Please use the provided calculator tool to verify your totals before submission. If you encounter an insufficient balance, do not withdraw funds personally; instead, contact the finance team to authorize a temporary overdraft.

We aim to maximize our resources while minimizing overhead costs. Therefore, employees should economize on non-essential items. When allocating budget for travel, choose economical options where possible. For instance, booking flights in advance can prevent paying a premium for last-minute changes. Remember to subtract any personal meals from your claim.

The remainder of the proceeds from our quarterly fundraise will be used to subsidize training programs. We must approximate our needs carefully to avoid inflating the budget unnecessarily. It is probable that the external auditor will conduct a thorough review next month, so please ensure all entries are economically sound. Finally, if you need to adjust a transaction, update the spreadsheet immediately and notify your banker of any pending withdrawal requests. Note that unused office equipment will be sold at auction to offset these costs.
